The defining feature of paraconsistent logic within a therapeutic architecture is its formal rejection of the **Principle of Explosion**. ### Logics of Formal Inconsistency (LFIs) as Logical Circuit Breakers

To operationalize paraconsistent logic within an RTA, systems engineers employ a specialized subclass of non-classical logics known as **Logics of Formal Inconsistency (LFIs)**. LFIs internalize the metatheoretical concept of "consistency" directly into the object language of the system by introducing a unary **consistency operator** ($\circ$). 

The formula $\circ P$ formally states: *"The proposition $P$ is consistent"*. Within an LFI, the principle of explosion is weakened to the **principle of gentle explosion**:
$$\{ P, \neg P, \circ P \} \vdash Q$$
If a proposition is explicitly asserted to be consistent ($\circ P$), and yet a contradiction ($P \land \neg P$) is encountered, the system explodes classically. However, if the consistency operator is false ($\neg \circ P$), the contradiction does not trigger explosion.

This construct acts as a **"logical circuit breaker"** within the RTA. When an inconsistency involving $P$ is detected, the LFI engine disables explosive inference rules specifically for reasoning paths that reference $P$. This surgically quarantines the contradiction, allowing the rest of the system's **Semantic Genome Architecture (SGA)** to continue operating safely under classical logic rules.
