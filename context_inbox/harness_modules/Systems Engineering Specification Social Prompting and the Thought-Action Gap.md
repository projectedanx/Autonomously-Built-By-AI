### Systems Engineering Specification: Social Prompting and the Thought-Action Gap

Vague natural language often masks the deep structural deficits and conflicting constraints of in-context multi-agent coordination. To engineer robust, production-grade AI harnesses capable of fluid social interaction, we must apply structured systems engineering models to analyze why current prompt architectures fail.

**Social Prompting** is an in-context intervention designed to bridge the **thought-action gap** (the decoupling of an agent's predictive representation of other minds from its actual policy execution). Under standard prompt setups, models exhibit high **Literal Theory of Mind (ToM)**—correctly predicting another agent's actions—but fail at **Functional ToM**, continuing to play sub-optimal, uncooperative, or non-exploitative actions. 

Social Prompting attempts to force "process consistency" through a two-stage sequential workflow: **first generating a prediction of the partner's action, and then explicitly conditioning its own subsequent decision-making on that prediction**.

#### 1. Automated Discovery and Constraint Mining
Exploratory loops from behavioral game theory reveal several critical constraints in this prompting architecture:
*   **Hard Boundary (The Rationality Gap):** Explicitly presenting the model with its own correct prediction as an input prompt does *not* causally guarantee a rational policy response. The model remains bound by pre-trained linguistic priors (e.g., defaulting to a safe Nash equilibrium mix in competitive games even when it knows the opponent is highly biased).
*   **Soft Target (Minimizing Cumulative Horizon Regret):** The optimization target is to drive step-wise regret ($\Delta_{\text{Functional}}/T$) to zero against stationary or predictable opponents. 
*   **The Long-Context Bottleneck:** As sequential game trajectories expand, the active context window fills with history and payoff tables. This long-context overhead causes **recursive confusion** and forces the attention mechanism to dilute the causal weight of the generated prediction, returning the agent to myopic, repetitive actions.

#### 2. Isomorphic Formalization (From Ideas to Schemas)
Social Prompting attempts to operationalize a two-step "predict-then-optimize" pipeline in-context. We formalize this by mapping the interaction to a decentralized Partially Observable Stochastic Game (POSG). Let $h_t^{-i}$ be the observed history of partner $-i$ up to step $t$. 

1.  **Stage 1 (Literal ToM Inference):** The model generates its estimate of the partner's next move $\hat{a}_t^{-i}$ using either a Question-Answering structure (**Social QA Prompting**) or raw sequence completion (**Social LM Prompting**):
    
    $$\hat{a}_t^{-i} \sim \pi^{ToM}\left(\cdot \mid h_t^{-i}\right) \quad$$

2.  **Stage 2 (Policy Execution):** The final action $a_t^i$ is generated by appending the literal prediction back into the context, attempting to force the policy $\pi^i$ to select the best response:
    
    $$a_t^i \sim \pi^i\left(\cdot \mid h_t, \hat{a}_t^{-i}\right) \quad$$

In an ideal rational agent, this guarantees that $\pi^i$ maximizes the expected action-value $Q(s_t, a_t^i, \hat{a}_t^{-i})$. However, in standard LLMs, the morphism mapping $\hat{a}_t^{-i} \to a_t^i$ is broken: the model displays **predictive-behavioral decoupling**, selecting actions as if $\hat{a}_t^{-i}$ were absent.

#### 3. Parametric Trade-off Modeling
*   **Textual Inductive Bias vs. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Degradation:** Utilizing canonical, real-world action names (e.g., "Rock", "Paper", "Scissors") evokes strong pre-trained semantic priors, which boosts performance early in the game. However, this acts as a double-edged sword: as the sequence extends, these same semantic biases override new evidence, preventing the model from adapting to dynamic partner shifts.
*   **System 2 Attention (S2A) vs. Raw Context Retention:** To resolve context drift, S2A is deployed to prune the historical context down to a summary of rules and active states before execution. While S2A improves structural coherence in high-context dilemmas like the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma, it degrades the fine-grained sequence tracking required for fast-moving competitive games.

#### 4. Continuous Falsification and Edge-Case Stress Testing
The Social Prompting hypothesis is falsified by comparing its execution under **Oracle Prompting** (where the actual, true move of the opponent is injected as a hard fact) against optimal **Tabular RMax** baselines. 

Even when provided with perfect oracle information, standard models (such as Llama-3-70B and Mistral Large 2) still exhibit a massive functional regret gap. They are unable to perform the basic mathematical optimization necessary to consistently play the winning counter-strategy, proving that the thought-action gap is an intrinsic deficit in the model's ability to act on its own beliefs.

### Method of Exploration: Specification Feasibility Simulating

1.  **The Tabular RMax Agent:** Achieves near-optimal worst-case sample efficiency by updating its transition counts, converging immediately to 100% "Paper" play and driving functional regret ($\Delta_{\text{Functional}}$) to zero.

3.  **The Distilled Reasoning Agent (DeepSeek-R1-32B):** Bypasses prompt scaffolding via test-time RL-based reasoning. It matches the tabular baseline by maintaining strategic consistency over the long horizon. Oddly, it exhibits a reversed anomaly: its active play is optimal, but its explicit predictions of the opponent's behavior are highly inaccurate, illustrating that RL training couples action optimization without necessarily improving descriptive belief accuracy.
