### Section 1: Kuhnian Stage 1 — The FRW Disciplinary Matrix (Normal Science)

Thomas Kuhn defines **Normal Science** as systematic, cumulative puzzle-solving conducted within the boundaries of an accepted paradigm. In modern cosmology, this disciplinary matrix is the **Friedmann-Robertson-Walker (FRW)** metric. 

#### 1. The "Spherical Cow" of Cosmology
The FRW metric simplifies Einstein's ten coupled, non-linear partial differential equations—which govern the complex feedback loops between the local matter tensor ($T_{ab}$) and the gravitational spacetime metric ($g_{ab}$)—by enforcing the **Cosmological Principle**: the assumption that the universe is perfectly and eternally homogeneous and isotropic. This "spherical cow" assumption collapses Einstein’s non-linear field equations into a simple, analytically solvable system of two ordinary differential equations known as **Friedmann's Equations**:

$$\dot{a}^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho a^2 - kc^2 \left( + \frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}a^2 \right)$$

$$\dot{\rho} + 3\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\left(\rho + \frac{p}{c^2}\right) = 0$$

Under this idealized model, the entire dynamic evolution of the cosmos is reduced to a single degree of freedom: the **cosmic scale-factor** ($a(t)$), which dictates a smooth, universal "cosmic time" ($t$) that is assumed to tick identically everywhere. 

#### 2. The Normal Science Workflow
For decades, cosmologists have engaged in normal scientific puzzle-solving by treating the FRW metric as a sacrosanct background geometry. When simulating structure formation, researchers use large **N-body simulations** to track gravitational clumping. However, to make these simulations computationally tractable, they rely on **Newtonian gravitation**, which is linear in the gravitational potential and assumes a globally valid, smooth background space. This linear simplification assumes that small local irregularities do not alter the dynamical expansion rate of the universe at larger scales.
