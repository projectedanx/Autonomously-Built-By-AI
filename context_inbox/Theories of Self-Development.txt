Self-development happens in early childhood stages.
Getting "stuck" in a stage can cause emotional and psychological problems in adulthood.
Personality develops throughout the entire lifespan (8 stages from birth to death).
Emphasized the social aspects of development—balancing personal desires with society's expectations.
The self develops by negotiating between one's internal world and the social world.
Social relationships are crucial for healthy development.

The Looking-Glass Self.
Our sense of self is built from how we perceive others see us.
The "Self".

Monkeys raised in isolation showed disturbed behavior.
When given a choice, monkeys preferred a soft, cloth "mother" for comfort over a wire mother that provided food.
Emotional comfort and attachment are as important as basic needs like food.
Preparatory Stage:
Children imitate others without understanding the actions.
Play Stage:
Children take on the role of one other person at a time (e.g., playing "mom" or "doctor").
Game Stage:
Children learn to understand multiple roles and how they interact simultaneously (e.g., understanding all the different jobs in a restaurant).
Generalized Other:
The final stage where an individual understands the common behavioral expectations of society as a whole.
This is when a person has developed a "self" from a sociological view.
Preconventional:
Young children experience the world through their senses; morality is about avoiding punishment.
Conventional:
Develops in teen years; people become aware of others' feelings and follow rules to maintain social order.
Postconventional:
People think about morality in abstract terms (e.g., universal rights).
They recognize that what is legal is not always what is moral.
Focuses on rules and laws.
Socialized for a work environment where rules are key.
Considers the reasons behind actions and focuses on relationships.
Socialized for a home environment where flexibility and nurturing are important.
