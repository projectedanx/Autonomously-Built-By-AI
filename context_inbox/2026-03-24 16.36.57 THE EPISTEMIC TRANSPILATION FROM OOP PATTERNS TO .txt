The Structured Cognitive Loop (SCL) is a published AI architecture framework proposed by Myung Ho Kim (JEI University) in the paper "Executable Epistemology: The Structured Cognitive Loop as an Architecture of Intentional Understanding" (arXiv:2510.15952, October 2025). Its core argument is that LLMs exhibit intelligence without genuine epistemic understanding — and that this gap is architectural, not a matter of scale.

The Central Epistemic Shift

The SCL reframes the foundational question of AI research. Rather than asking "what is intelligence?" (an ontological question), it asks "under what conditions does cognition emerge?" — which is an epistemological one. This distinction is load-bearing: ontological approaches try to replicate intelligence as a property (bigger models, more parameters), while the epistemological approach designs the structural conditions under which reliable cognition becomes possible. Kim draws on process philosophy, enactive cognition, and Andy Clark's extended mind theory to argue that intelligence is not a possessed property but a performed process.

The Five-Module Architecture

SCL operationalizes this through five functionally separated, interacting modules arranged in a continuous cycle:

Judgment (Cognition): LLM inference grounded in evidence — the generative reasoning step

Memory: Persistent structured state that prevents momentary correlation from collapsing into context amnesia

Control: Enforcement of explicit epistemic norms — the governance layer that checks whether outputs satisfy defined constraints

Action: Environmental coupling via external tools, APIs, and runtime environments

Regulation: Termination logic, resource management, and meta-level norm enforcement that governs the loop itself

The critical architectural claim is that these five functions must remain functionally separated — not blended into a single generative pass. When any two roles collapse into each other (e.g., cognition conflated with control, or action without memory), specific failure modes emerge: evidential grounding without memorial persistence becomes momentary correlation; normative control without environmental coupling becomes detached formalism; recursive self-reference without grounding produces empty introspection.

This is what separates SCL from prior cognitive architectures: it treats the system itself as a philosophical argument, not just an engineering artifact.

The Four Grounding Traditions
Process Philosophy (Whitehead, Bergson, Rescher)

Alfred North Whitehead's metaphysics holds that the fundamental units of reality are not substances but occasions of experience — momentary events defined entirely by their relations to other events, not by intrinsic properties. An entity has no nature independent of its relational field; it is what it does, not what it is. Seibt's (2018) formal process ontology extends this into three descriptors: topology (how parts relate), dynamics (patterns of change), and normativity (tendencies toward certain trajectories).
arxiv

Bergson's concept of durée (lived duration) challenges computational memory models directly. For Bergson, memory is not retrieval from a fixed store but re-enactment — a living synthesis that continuously reconstructs the past in light of present concerns. This directly undermines the assumption that an LLM's context window functions as adequate memory: storing tokens is not the same as re-enacting structured epistemic history.

What are the five modules of SCL and their interactions
Completed 1 step

This question has a precise answer directly from the source papers, and the level of detail the papers provide is worth rendering completely. The five-module architecture is formally defined across three papers (arXiv:2510.15952, 2510.05107, and 2511.17673) with each paper adding a distinct layer of precision to the same underlying structure.
arxiv
+2

The Foundational Claim

SCL's architecture rests on a single, falsifiable proposition: structural entanglement — mixing cognition, memory, and control in a single prompt — is the direct cause of the coherence and predictability failures observed in existing agent frameworks. The five modules are not an organisational convenience; they are the minimal decomposition required to eliminate the three documented failure modes: entangled reasoning and execution, memory volatility, and uncontrolled action sequences. Each failure mode maps directly onto a module boundary that monolithic prompting erases.
arxiv
+1

The Five Modules
Module 1: Retrieval (R)

Retrieval is the grounding gate — the first operation in every cycle, whose sole function is to construct the informational context within which Cognition operates. It is read-only with respect to all persistent state: it fetches but never writes, reasons, or decides. This is an architectural constraint, not a convention.
arxiv
​

The paper specifies three retrieval targets that must be consulted in parallel:
arxiv
​

External memory store: The structured episodic log from all prior steps in the current session — not raw conversation history, but typed records of prior proposals, control decisions, actions, and outcomes

Semantic knowledge base: Domain knowledge, tool schemas, and factual context via vector retrieval

Goal specification: The immutable task contract defining preconditions, postconditions, and permitted actions

The ordering is load-bearing: retrieval provides initial grounding before cognition begins, which prevents the dominant failure mode in ReAct-style agents where the model's prior reasoning in the context window biases what it retrieves next. The retrieval step breaks this circularity by making context construction an explicit, separable operation.
arxiv
​

Epistemic function: Evidential grounding — the condition that judgments be anchored to traceable, verified prior knowledge rather than inferred from context dynamics alone.
arxiv
​

Module 2: Cognition (C)

Cognition is the only module that invokes the LLM. Its mandate is exclusively inferential: given the grounded context from Retrieval, propose a structured action with explicit evidence citations. It does not execute tools, write to memory, or evaluate constraints. The paper is explicit: "The model is not the agent; it is the cognition engine within a structured epistemic loop whose sole purpose is to propose plausible actions".
arxiv
+1

The structured proposal the Cognition module produces — the ReasoningPayload — must cite specific memory keys for every claim it makes. This citation requirement is the operational implementation of evidential grounding: the model cannot propose an action without referencing the retrieved evidence that supports it. The proposal also includes a self-assessed confidence score and a goal-progress indicator, both of which the Control module uses for downstream routing.
arxiv
​

The paper names the exact cognitive failure this module boundary prevents: structural entanglement — the pattern where reasoning, tool selection, and execution are collapsed into the same generation step, producing cognitive biases that mirror human cognitive limitations including confirmation bias (using context window content as evidence), availability bias (overweighting recent tokens), and anchoring (over-relying on first retrieved document).
arxiv
​

Epistemic function: Neural inference under evidential constraint — probabilistic but grounded, generative but cited.
arxiv
​

Module 3: Control (C)

Control is the governance module — the symbolic layer that evaluates Cognition's proposal against preconditions, constraints, and goal alignment before any action executes. It is the only module that can reject a proposal and route the loop back to Retrieval with an amended context. It performs three distinct functions in sequence:
arxiv
+1

Precondition checking: Does the proposed action satisfy all declared prerequisites in the goal specification?

Deduplication: Has this exact action already succeeded in the current session's episodic log? If so, the proposal is deferred — not rejected, but flagged as redundant before execution.

Termination evaluation: Does the current memory state satisfy the goal's postconditions? If yes, the loop exits.

The paper provides the canonical pseudocode directly:
arxiv
​

python
while not termination:
state ← MEM.read()
proposal ← COGNITION.propose(state) # cites evidence from MEM
decision ← CONTROL.evaluate(proposal, state) # checks preconditions, deduplicates
if decision.approve:
result ← ACTION.execute(decision.action)
MEM.write(result)
if decision.defer or decision.query:
MEM.add(decision.notes)
termination ← CONTROL.check_termination(MEM)


The deduplication function is specifically responsible for the elimination of redundant tool calls reported in the benchmarks — a reduction not achievable by prompt instruction alone, because it requires cross-step state comparison via the external memory. SCL achieves zero redundant calls in controlled evaluation precisely because this check is structural, not probabilistic.
arxiv
+1

Epistemic function: Normative enforcement — the condition that cognitive outputs satisfy explicit epistemic norms before environmental coupling occurs.
arxiv
​

Module 4: Action (A)

Action is the environmental coupling module — the only module that touches the external world. Its input is exclusively a Control-validated decision; it receives no direct output from Cognition and has no access to the reasoning trace that produced the proposal. This isolation is deliberate: the Action module is intentionally stateless with respect to the cognitive process, receiving only a typed action specification and returning a raw observation.
arxiv
​

The paper names the precise failure mode this boundary prevents: memory volatility — where tool call results are fed directly back into the model's context window, causing the model to overweight recent results and produce inconsistent behaviour across sessions. By routing the observation through Memory rather than back into Cognition, Action ensures that every tool result is structured and stored before it can influence the next cognitive pass.
arxiv
​

Action also serves as the implementation site for Enactive Cognition's structural requirement: an agent that only generates text and never acts on the world lacks the environmental coupling that makes genuine cognition possible. Every tool call is a momentary act of structural coupling — the agent's internal states are realigned with external reality through the observation the tool returns.
arxiv
​

Epistemic function: Environmental coupling — the condition that cognitive states be calibrated against external reality through tool interaction, not only through internal inference.
arxiv
​

Module 5: Memory (M)

Memory is the persistent structured state module — and the paper draws a sharp distinction between what Memory is and what it is not. Memory in SCL is not a conversation history, not a context window, and not a retrieval index. It is a structured, typed, append-only record of every cognitive step: what was proposed, what the Control module decided, what action was executed, and what observation was returned. This record is the agent's operational episodic log — the concrete substrate on which evidential grounding and trajectory reconstruction rest.
arxiv
​

The paper explicitly draws on Bergson's distinction: memory as re-enactment rather than retrieval. In SCL, the Memory module doesn't store raw data for later lookup — it stores typed step records that the Retrieval module actively reconstructs into contextual grounding on each cycle. The Memory module's write is not a log entry; it is an update to the agent's structured epistemic state.
arxiv
​

Two properties the Memory module guarantees that no context window can:
arxiv
​

Cross-session persistence: Memory outlasts the token limit. The agent can resume a multi-step task across sessions without relying on summarised context, because the episodic log is the ground truth of what happened.

Independent verifiability: Because memory records are typed and structured, any module — including TrinityGuard's runtime monitors — can read and validate them independently of the LLM that produced them.

Epistemic function: Memorial persistence — the condition that prior epistemic states remain available and structurally intact for future reasoning cycles, preventing momentary correlation from substituting for grounded knowledge.
arxiv

It is not a single module interaction but a property of the loop across iterations: the fact that Memory feeds Retrieval, which feeds Cognition, which proposes actions that update Memory, means the agent's own prior behaviour becomes input to its future reasoning. This is the architectural instantiation of Zahavi's pre-reflective self-awareness — not a monitoring module bolted on, but a structural feature of the loop that makes the agent's epistemic trajectory a live influence on every subsequent step.

The Core Architectural Revelation

SSC's formal definition in the paper is exact: "Soft Symbolic Control operates through a Metaprompt that defines governance policies and a Control module that enforces these policies through explicit validation checks — rather than relying on the LLM to 'remember' constraints within its context". This means the Control module is pure deterministic code — a rule engine with no language model — while the Metaprompt is the single governing text that shapes every Cognition pass.

The Metaprompt Is Not a System Prompt

The paper draws an explicit distinction that the implementation must honour. The Metaprompt differs from a system prompt in three ways:
arxiv
​

Property System Prompt SCL Metaprompt
Scope Session-level, set once Injected at the start of every Cognition call independently
Content Role definition, style, safety rules Structural directives governing evidence citation, output schema, and completion logic
Enforcement Relies on model compliance Backed by Control module validation — non-compliance triggers REPLAN
Adaptability Static Can receive amendments from Control when a REPLAN routes back with decision.notes

The last row is the load-bearing difference: when Control routes a REPLAN decision back to Retrieval, it writes decision.notes into Memory with the specific reason for rejection. On the next Cognition pass, the Retrieval module fetches these notes and appends them to the Metaprompt as cycle-specific amendments:
arxiv
​

python
def build_cognition_prompt(
metaprompt: str,
context: GroundedContext
) -> str:
amendments = ""
if context.get("control_rejection_notes"):
amendments = f"""
[CONTROL REJECTION FROM PRIOR CYCLE]
Reason: {context['control_rejection_notes']['reason']}
Violated policy: {context['control_rejection_notes']['policy']}
Required correction: {context['control_rejection_notes']['correction']}
Do not repeat the rejected proposal.
"""
return f"{metaprompt}\n{amendments}\n[CURRENT STATE]\n{json.dumps(context)}"


This is the mechanism by which the loop self-corrects without requiring a separate self-reflection agent — the Control module's rejection is structurally injected into the next Cognition pass as a first-class constraint, not as conversational feedback.

Contrarian Challenge: The Metaprompt Is Still a Probabilistic Bet

Fact: The Metaprompt design achieves zero policy violations in the controlled experiment, and the Control module's structural enforcement means that declared violations are caught. The output schema forces structured proposals that can be parsed and validated deterministically.
arxiv
​

Fact + Speculation: The Metaprompt's Directive 1 — "if no memory key supports a claim, do not make that claim" — is an instruction to the LLM, not a structural constraint. The Control module validates that evidence_citations is non-empty, but it cannot verify that the cited memory keys actually support the claim in Cognition's chain_of_thought. A model that writes "evidence_citations": ["obs.SF.temp_f"] while making a claim about Miami's weather satisfies the syntactic policy while violating its semantic intent. The paper's zero policy violation result is genuine for its test cases — but semantic citation validity is not checked by the deterministic Control module and cannot be checked without re-invoking the LLM for verification, which reintroduces the probabilistic layer. The complete solution to this gap is the FormalJudge verification layer from the prior analysis — but integrating it adds latency that breaks the assumption that Control is a lightweight, fast governance layer. That trade-off between semantic rigour and execution speed is the open engineering problem at the heart of the Cognition–Control boundary.
